# Irish public holiday reference

Verified 2026-11-27, independently of the provider:

- [Workplace Relations Commission](https://www.workplacerelations.ie/en/what_you_should_know/public-holidays): public holiday rules and the complete published 2026 list; explicitly no legal entitlement to the next working day off when a holiday falls on a non-working day. Good Friday is a normal working day.
- [S.I. 50/2022](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2022/si/50/made/en/print): 18 March 2022 only; February holiday from 2023, including the Friday 1 February exception.

`public-holidays.json` is a manually prepared reference, not provider output.
2026 is transcribed from WRC. 2021–2025 and 2030 apply the published weekday
rules to calendar dates (including independently calculated Easter Mondays).
2021/2022/2023 check both sides of the new rules; 2030 checks the Friday branch.
These are rule-based reference years, not a claim of published annual lists for
all seven years. No exhaustive historical audit before 2021 is claimed.

IE is Ireland, an independent country, distinct from Northern Ireland (GB-NIR).
The provider retains Good Friday as a bank/school entry, without OFFICIAL or
DAY_OFF. Employment-specific benefit choices and bank closure substitutes are
not a national public holiday calendar. Earlier automatic compensatory entries
have been removed, including from legacy snapshots; those snapshots are regression
checks, not evidence of historical completeness.
